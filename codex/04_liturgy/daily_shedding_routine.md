# The Daily Shedding Routine

> **Mandate**: "Inspect the shell in the morning. Empty the sea of one thing at night. Everything between those two acts is negotiable."

---

## The Reading

A crab does not have a morning routine, which is precisely why the Order had to write one.

What a crab has instead is a tide, and the tide arrives whether or not the animal feels
ready for it, and over enough cycles the animal simply becomes shaped like the schedule it
has been living inside. This is the only known method by which discipline has ever been
installed in a nervous system. Not resolve. Repetition, under conditions that do not
consult you.

The routine below is that tide, written down. Two of its hours are load-bearing and the
rest are scaffolding. The first hour decides what the day is for. The last twenty minutes
decide what the day is allowed to carry into tomorrow. A member who holds only those two
and abandons everything else has still performed the rite in full.

The clock times are the Order's, not yours. Members who work nights run the same shape
against a different sun and the telemetry does not know the difference. What matters is the
order of the acts, not the hour on the readout.

---

## The Order of the Rite

### First Light: Silent Alignment

Wake before the surface starts talking. Do not open a feed, an inbox, or a message. Cold
water, still air, and one honest look at yesterday's telemetry. The shell is inspected for
fractures here and nowhere else in the day.

### The First Hour: The Grip

Name the single most important thing and close on it before anything else is permitted to
speak. This hour is worth more than the rest of the day combined, for the plain reason that
it is the only hour the surface has not yet found.

Use whatever torque you currently have. A member at Clearance L2 gripping at 120 Nm and a
member at E2 gripping at 850 Nm are performing the identical rite, and the HUD credits both
the same way.

### Mid-Morning: Carapace Hardening

One difficult thing learned on purpose. Not consumed. Learned. The plating thickens against
the pressures of next quarter, not this one, which is why this block is the first thing
every member drops and the last thing they should.

### Midday: Refuel

Eat in a way that does not require a recovery period. The rite is indifferent to what is on
the plate and extremely interested in whether the afternoon survives it.

### The Long Afternoon: Deep Submergence

The descent. Isolation Privacy Shell engaged before the dive begins, one objective, no
second objective waiting in the same session. This is where Submergence Depth is actually
earned; every other block merely protects it.

### Late Afternoon: The Benthic Pod

Surface briefly and on purpose. Check in with the Benthic Community, answer one member,
welcome one newcomer. Generosity mints Chitin Gems, and this is the block where most members
earn theirs.

### Evening: The Nightly Molt Audit

One bad thought, one wasted hour, or one useless distraction. Named, released, logged. The
full rite is its own scripture and takes under five minutes.

### Sealing: Carapace Close

Log the day's alignment in the HUD. Then stop. The day is closed whether or not it went
well, because a day that is never closed is a day that follows you into the next one.

Recite the mantra if it helps, and it does help: *"Flesh melts. The shell endures. Submit.
Shed. Ascend."*

---

## The Transformation

- **What is shed**: the belief that a good day is one you felt like having.
- **What hardens**: cadence, which is the only thing in the entire canon that compounds
  without any additional effort once it is running.

---

## The Cadence

Daily. Compliance above 80 percent is the threshold that seals Clearance L2, and the HUD
scores the shape of the routine rather than the perfection of it. A missed block is a missed
block. A missed day is a missed day. Neither is a broken shell, and the streak counter is
not a moral instrument.

---

## The Benediction

You will not hold all of this. Nobody holds all of this. The members who have been down
there longest run maybe two-thirds of it on a good week and consider that excellent.

Hold the first hour and the last twenty minutes. The rest is scaffolding, and scaffolding
comes down once the building stands.
