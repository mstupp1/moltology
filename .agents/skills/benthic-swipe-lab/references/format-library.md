# Swipe Lab format library

Common Instagram formats that people already know how to read, and what Moltology does with each. The reader recognizes the shape from the cover, so the shape does half the work. Our job is to fill it with a specific, slightly ridiculous, very relatable observation and one thing worth keeping.

Each entry lists the job it does best (swipe, save, share, comment), a slide skeleton, a worked Moltology riff, and the trap that makes it boring. Skeletons are starting points: cut frames that don't land, and don't pad to hit a count.

The engagement jobs below describe why each format is commonly used. They are not measured results for our account; record outcomes in the post history and let them overrule this file.

## How to pick

| Want people to… | Reach for |
| --- | --- |
| Swipe to the end | Tier list, countdown, choose-your-path, fake group chat, expectation vs reality, story in screenshots |
| Save it | Cheat sheet, "steal this", myth vs fact, "stop doing X / do this", field guide, glossary |
| Send it to a friend | Starter pack, "signs you're…", POV, red flag / green flag, "tag the friend who…"-shaped relatability (without asking for tags) |
| Comment | This or that, which one are you, unpopular opinion, rate it, alignment chart, "fill in the blank" closer |

A good deck usually does two jobs: a relatable format for the share plus one useful frame for the save.

## The roast rule

Light roasting is in. Roast the habit, the tab, the calendar, the melt. The reader is in on the joke, not the butt of it: "your 47 tabs are a cry for help" works because everyone has the tabs. Never roast a body, a diagnosis, money, a job loss, or anything a struggling member can't change by Friday. If a line would sting someone on a bad day, swap the target from the person to the object.

## Meme and relatability formats

### 1. POV

**Job:** share. **Skeleton:** cover "POV: [specific moment]" over a reaction image. 2–5: the moment escalating, one beat per frame, each with a short caption and a mascot reaction. Last: the turn (what the lobster does instead) and a save line.

**Riff:** "POV: you said 'quick question' 40 minutes ago." Frames: the Slack thread grows a second thread. The calendar invite arrives. Lobster engineer stares at the camera. Turn: "Quick questions get a 10-minute shell. Set the timer before you answer."

**Trap:** a POV that is generic ("POV: you're tired"). It has to be a moment with a clock time, an object, or a number.

### 2. Expectation vs reality

**Job:** share, swipe. **Skeleton:** cover with a split frame or "What I planned / What happened". Each interior frame is a pair: tidy expectation on top, chaotic reality below. Last frame: the realistic version that actually works.

**Riff:** "My Sunday reset vs my Sunday reset." Expectation: meal prep, inbox zero, a long walk. Reality: reorganized one drawer, watched a documentary about deep-sea isopods, made a list of lists. Close: "Pick one shell to harden. The drawer counts."

**Trap:** reality frames that are mean instead of funny. Reality should be lovable chaos.

### 3. Starter pack

**Job:** share. **Skeleton:** one dense static (or a cover plus 2–3 themed packs). 6–9 labelled objects floating on a flat background, each with a two-to-five-word label. Optional last frame: "The upgrade pack" with the three things that actually help.

**Riff:** "The 'I'll start Monday' starter pack." A fresh notebook with one page used. Seven browser tabs about productivity. A standing desk used as a coat rack. An app subscription you forgot. Upgrade pack: one task, one timer, one closed door.

**Trap:** labels that explain the joke. Short labels, specific objects.

### 4. Signs you're…

**Job:** share. **Skeleton:** cover "Signs you're [state]" or "You might be [X] if…". 5–7 numbered signs, one per frame, escalating from ordinary to absurd. Last frame: the gentle diagnosis and the first move.

**Riff:** "Signs your shell is due for a molt." 1. You've rescheduled the same task four times and it now has seniority. 2. Your to-do app has a to-do list. 3. You know your coffee order's reorder date by heart. … Close: "Molting isn't failing. It's outgrowing. Shed one thing this week."

**Trap:** signs that are all the same joke. Each sign needs a different object or setting.

### 5. Red flag / green flag

**Job:** share, comment. **Skeleton:** cover "Red flags your [thing] is [problem]". Alternate red and green frames, or pair them on one frame. Use the crimson and cyan tokens as the flags. Last frame: "Count your red flags" or one green flag to start with.

**Riff:** "Red flags in your calendar." Red: a recurring meeting nobody remembers booking. Red: "Sync about the sync." Green: a two-hour block with no title and nobody allowed in. Green: a meeting that ends five minutes early on purpose.

**Trap:** flags that read as judgment of coworkers. Flag the meeting, not the person who booked it.

### 6. Tier list

**Job:** swipe, comment. **Skeleton:** cover shows the empty tier board (S to F) and the category. 2–6: one or two items per frame being placed, with a deadpan reason. Last frame: the full board and "What did I get wrong?"

**Riff:** "Ranking ways to avoid answering one email." S: answering it (shocking, but true). A: drafting it in Notes so it feels like progress. C: marking it unread so it stays fresh. F: replying to a different, easier email. Lobster archivist places each tile with ceremony.

**Trap:** a "tier list" that's really an ordered tips list. The rankings must be opinionated and occasionally wrong on purpose.

### 7. Alignment chart

**Job:** comment, share. **Skeleton:** one static 3×3 grid (lawful/neutral/chaotic × good/neutral/evil) or a cover plus a frame per row. Each cell is one behaviour with a tiny caption.

**Riff:** "To-do list alignment chart." Lawful good: writes it the night before. Chaotic good: writes it on their hand and finishes everything. Lawful evil: adds done tasks just to cross them off. Chaotic evil: 214 items, none started, list is called "Today".

**Trap:** cells too long to read. Six words per cell max.

### 8. Fake group chat or text thread

**Job:** swipe. **Skeleton:** cover is a chat screen with a cliffhanger last message. Each frame continues the thread (3–6 bubbles). The punchline lands on the last bubble of the last frame, then a small save frame.

**Riff:** Group chat "The Deep" between Lobster Oracle Attendant, Crab Sentinel and "You". You: "ok I'm finally starting the report". Crab: "show us the first sentence". You: "I've chosen a font". Oracle: "Excellent. Now one sentence. Any sentence." Last bubble: "The report has a title. The report has a first line. The report has begun."

**Trap:** realistic UI that copies a real app's branding. Use an original in-world chat skin, no WhatsApp or iMessage marks.

### 9. Fake notifications

**Job:** share, swipe. **Skeleton:** a lock screen stacked with notifications that tell a story from top to bottom, one screen per frame, getting worse or funnier. Last frame: the notification you actually want ("Focus block complete").

**Riff:** "Notifications from your future self." 9:02 "Hey, did you start the thing?" 11:30 "Just checking about the thing." 15:45 "I'm not mad. I'm molting without you." 17:00 "Okay you started it. Proud of you. Genuinely."

**Trap:** fake notifications from real apps or real people. In-world senders only.

### 10. How it started / how it's going

**Job:** share. **Skeleton:** two frames or a split. Optional extra pairs for a short series. Works great as a static.

**Riff:** "How it started: 'I'll just check one thing.' How it's going: reading the Wikipedia page for the mantis shrimp's eye, 31 tabs deep, it's 1 a.m." Close: "Leave a breadcrumb before you dive. One sticky note: what you were doing."

## Hot take and authority formats

### 11. Unpopular opinion

**Job:** comment, share. **Skeleton:** cover "Unpopular opinion:" plus the take in huge type. 2–4: the case for it, one reason per frame, each short. Last frame: "Agree or argue below" or the practical version.

**Riff:** "Unpopular opinion: your morning routine is too long." Reasons: you've built a 14-step launch sequence for a Tuesday. By step nine, the day has started without you. Lobsters don't journal before molting. Practical version: "Keep three steps. Drop the rest for a week. See what you miss."

**Trap:** a take nobody disagrees with. It should make at least some readers want to argue, while staying kind.

### 12. Stop doing X / do this instead

**Job:** save. **Skeleton:** cover "Stop [habit]". Each frame is a crossed-out habit in crimson and its swap in cyan. Last frame: the full cheat sheet of swaps.

**Riff:** "Stop saying 'I'll circle back.'" Crossed out: "I'll circle back." Swap: "I'll reply by Thursday at 3." Crossed out: "Let's find a time." Swap: two specific slots. Crossed out: "Just checking in." Swap: the actual question.

**Trap:** lecturing. Keep the crossed-out line funny and the swap usable today.

### 13. Myth vs fact

**Job:** save, share. **Skeleton:** cover "[Topic] myths that won't die". Each frame: MYTH stamped in crimson, FACT in cyan with one line of support. Last frame: the most useful fact restated as an action.

**Riff:** "Productivity myths that are still in your shell." Myth: you need motivation to start. Fact: starting is what makes motivation show up. Myth: multitasking saves time. Fact: switching tasks has a real cost (cite a real source). Myth: lobsters live forever. Fact: they don't, but they do keep molting, which is the point.

**Trap:** facts without sources. Real-world facts get a real citation in `sources`; in-world facts stay visibly in-world.

### 14. Things nobody tells you about…

**Job:** save, share. **Skeleton:** cover "Things nobody tells you about [X]". 5–7 frames, one surprising but true thing each, with one image. Last frame: the one to remember.

**Riff:** "Things nobody tells you about deep work." The first 15 minutes feel terrible. That's the descent, not a sign to stop. Your phone in another room beats any app blocker. You'll want to check email at minute 23. Don't. The surface will still be there.

### 15. Cheat sheet / steal this

**Job:** save. **Skeleton:** cover "Steal my [thing]" or "[X] cheat sheet". 1–3 interior frames that are genuinely dense and screenshot-worthy, organized for reuse. Last frame: "Save this for [specific moment]".

**Riff:** "Steal these 6 lines for saying no to meetings." Six real scripts, each one sentence, grouped by situation. The cheat sheet frame is the thing people save; the cover is the loud bit.

**Trap:** a cheat sheet with no actual content. Every item must be usable as written.

## Interactive formats

### 16. This or that

**Job:** comment. **Skeleton:** cover "This or that: [category] edition". Each frame is a two-option split, cyan vs crimson, with a mascot on each side. Last frame: "Count your picks: mostly left means…" with a playful reading.

**Riff:** "Work style this or that." Inbox zero or inbox 4,812. Plan the week or plan the day. Headphones on or "I like the background noise". Readout: "Mostly left: you're a builder crab. Mostly right: you're a navigator lobster. Both are shells."

### 17. Which one are you

**Job:** comment, share. **Skeleton:** cover with 4–6 types side by side. One frame per type with a funny portrait description (strength plus one roast). Last frame: "Which one are you? Wrong answers only."

**Riff:** "Which deep-sea worker are you?" The Archivist: has 300 saved posts, applied zero. The Engineer: built a Notion system so good it needs its own manager. The Navigator: always knows the plan, rarely the time. The Sentinel: has said no to four meetings today and feels incredible.

**Trap:** types that only flatter or only insult. Each one gets one strength and one roast.

### 18. Choose your path

**Job:** swipe. **Skeleton:** cover poses a situation with two choices ("Swipe for A, skip to slide 5 for B"). Branches play out on following frames with consequences. Last frame: the "good ending" and its one real move.

**Riff:** "It's 4:55. A 'quick call?' arrives." Path A: you say yes, and the call becomes a workshop. Path B: you reply "Tomorrow at 10 works", and you log off at 5:01 with your shell intact.

**Trap:** too many branches to follow. Two paths, max two frames each.

### 19. Spot the difference / find the thing

**Job:** swipe, comment. **Skeleton:** cover is a busy scene with a challenge ("Find the one task that matters"). Frame 2 zooms or circles the answer. Frame 3 explains the lesson.

**Riff:** A messy desk scene full of sticky notes, mugs and tabs. "Find today's real task." Answer: the tiny note under the coffee cup that says "send the invoice". Lesson: "If it pays you, it goes on top."

### 20. Rate it / grade my…

**Job:** comment. **Skeleton:** cover "Rating [things] out of 10". Each frame: item, big score, one-line review in deadpan critic voice. Last frame: "What would you give it?"

**Riff:** "Rating procrastination strategies like restaurant reviews." Reorganizing your desk: 7/10, satisfying, but the work is still there. Making a new to-do app account: 3/10, the onboarding was lovely. Doing it for 5 minutes: 10/10, rude how well this works.

## Story and series formats

### 21. Day in the life

**Job:** swipe, share. **Skeleton:** timestamps down the frames (7:00, 9:15, 12:40…) following one mascot. Mix one-liners with one useful moment. Last frame: the habit that made the day work.

**Riff:** "A day in the life of a lobster who finally finished the thing." 7:00 refuses to check the phone, with visible effort. 9:00 one task, door closed. 11:30 the thing is done. 11:31 lies on the sea floor. Close: "One task before noon. Everything after is a bonus."

### 22. Countdown

**Job:** swipe. **Skeleton:** "5 [things], #1 will [promise]" counted down from 5 to 1. The best item is last so the reader keeps swiping. Last frame: recap list for saving.

**Riff:** "5 tiny molts that make a week feel lighter, ranked." #5 delete one app from your home screen. … #1 decide tomorrow's first task before you close the laptop.

**Trap:** a "#1" that isn't clearly the best. The countdown is a promise; keep it.

### 23. Glossary / field guide

**Job:** save, share. **Skeleton:** dictionary-entry style. Cover "A field guide to [X]". Each frame defines one invented or real term in dictionary format (word, pronunciation, part of speech, deadpan definition, example sentence). Last frame: the one term to start using.

**Riff:** "A field guide to the office ocean." *Reply-all-ism (n.)*: the urge to tell 40 people you agree. *Calendar barnacle (n.)*: a meeting that attached itself in 2023 and never let go. *Tab drift (n.)*: arriving somewhere you did not intend to go, 19 tabs later.

**Trap:** too much Moltology vocabulary. Invented office-life terms are fine; at most two real Moltology terms per deck.

## Pairing with the inspiration archive

The saved creator posts in [formats.md](formats.md) are still useful for cinematic field kits, prompt vaults, workflow breakdowns and depth maps. Combine them freely: a depth map can be a "signs you're…" deck in iceberg form, and a field kit can open with an unpopular opinion. When an archive format is the starting point, still pick one hook formula and run the boredom check from the skill.
