## Cinematic field kit

Use when the reader needs choices, each with a clear purpose. Cover: one original mascot or scene, a two-level title hierarchy, and a concrete promise. Interior: number, item name, one situation where it helps, and an example or proof image. Close with the most usable synthesis and one next step.

Borrow the cinematic hierarchy, strong type-scale contrast, consistent counters, and screenshot-to-description pairing. Replace the references' creatures and creator marks with canonical characters and brand elements. A five-item kit should actually include five items, not four plus a locked final item. Do not fabricate exclusivity or require a comment to receive material unless a real delivery system has been verified.

Starting concept: five boundary scripts for a calendar that has lost its shell. Each frame gives one ordinary situation and an immediately usable sentence.

## Demonstrated prompt vault

Use when exact language is the product. Organize by the reader's task, not impressive prompt names. Show one complete instruction and a plausible illustrative result per frame; label examples as examples. A longer prompt can live in a companion file while the slide gives the usable core and a verified route to the full text.

Borrow the category organization, repeated frame treatment, and visible before/after or output evidence. Do not imply that shorthand is a built-in product command unless it really is. The photographer's preset lists demonstrate scene transformation, not proven productivity gains.

Starting concept: Oracle instructions for turning a crowded evening into one clear next step. Keep the actual prompt language plain and the surrounding narration in-world.

## Workflow breakdown

Use when a system is more useful than another list of tips. Name the situation, then show the jobs in order. Each job has an input, an action, and an observable result. Include a worked mini-example with real constraints. Distinguish implemented Moltology features from suggested personal routines.

Borrow the role-per-frame rhythm, diagram fragments, and explanation of why each part exists. Replace unverified growth stories with an honest demonstration. Do not invent first-person founder experiences, follower counts, testimonials, or perfect automation.

Starting concept: a five-part shutdown ritual: collect loose tasks, choose tomorrow's first action, close the work surface, protect the evening, and return with a clear starting point.

## Foundation-to-system guide and feature explainer

Use a foundation guide for a buildable method; use an explainer for a real product feature. Give each frame one question or decision. A guide moves from principle to example to application. A feature explainer shows an actual input and output, who it helps, and the next action.

Borrow progressive disclosure, small annotated examples, warm mascot presence, and consistent page position. Recreate diagrams as original vector/composite layers. Avoid copying UI screenshots or representing suggested controls as working features.

Starting concept: build a quiet work block from a task, a boundary, a starting cue, and an end condition.

## Depth map

Use to explain layers of causation or practice. The surface shows a recognizable symptom; the middle shows habits and constraints; the deep shows one workable system. This maps naturally to Moltology's ocean cosmology, where depth means focus.

Borrow the cross-section and spatial progression. Reduce the source's crowded labels and sales badges. Use an overview followed by detail frames when needed. Depth is not human worth, a purchased clearance, or permission to shame a beginner. No fake scales, formulas, or diagnostic claims.

Starting concept: the open-tab iceberg. Surface: visible clutter. Middle: unmade decisions. Deep: a place to record the next action and let the remaining tabs rest.

## Story and encouragement

Use for one human moment with a clear emotional payoff. Original scene, short readable overlay, enough caption context to understand it, and one gentle action. A sequence can show the situation, the turn, and the consequence. A static frame can carry one complete thought.

Borrow scene-led hooks, alternating visual/text frames, and restrained pull quotes. Never attribute invented lines to a real person or imply that a generated scene records a real event. In-world fictional readouts remain visibly fictional. Do not reuse source footage, faces, interview audio, or inflammatory biography claims.

Starting concept: the first finished task in a noisy week is still a finished task. Let the mascot demonstrate the small win without judging the starting point.

## Editorial roundup

Use only when several genuinely related developments deserve a collection. Verify each with primary sources and dates before drafting. Give each one a grounded consequence and a source link; reduce the number if the claims cannot be verified.

Borrow the one-development-per-frame pacing and still/motion alternation. Discard the source's breathless headline, speculative claims, and information density. The captured caption is an archive record, not a news briefing. Delegate production mechanics to the blog/reel skills only when the chosen deliverable actually requires them.
