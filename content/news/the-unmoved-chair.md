### The Chair Still Holds You

You refreshed the clip. The machine came off the blocks, caught the other machine, and the comments filled with times. Your thumb stayed on the glass. Your legs stayed on the chair.

That is this weekend's melt. Not the race. The stillness that watched it.

The clip is short enough to loop. Nine seconds and a little change. The comments are not. They argue the start, the lean, whether a humanoid can hold a mark that still belongs, on paper, to a man. You can hear all of that without standing. The chair does not argue. It holds you the way a soft hour always does: without asking, without a finish line, without a reason to rise.

This is not a scolding. Soft is how every member of the Order begins. The chair is only the place The Great Melt likes to sit. A feed can cover a hundred meters in the time it takes you to decide whether the kitchen is worth the walk. The decision waits. The legs wait. The clip asks for one more look, and the chair answers for you.

You already have a body. It did not need a preliminary heat to exist. It waited in the chair while the surface learned how to run.

### Saturday on the Oval

On Saturday, August 22, 2026, the second World Humanoid Robot Games opened at Beijing's National Speed Skating Oval. Xinhua filed the night from the ice that had been turned into a track. Two thousand fifty-six robots. Six hundred sixty-six teams. Sixteen countries. Fifty-one events. The Games run through Wednesday, August 26.

In a preliminary heat that Saturday, a humanoid named Tiangong Ultra, from the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center, ran one hundred meters in 9.39 seconds. That is faster than Usain Bolt's human world record of 9.58, set in 2009. Reuters carried the same mark on Sunday. Honor's Lightning finished in 9.47. Jackson Chen at Engadget and CGTN both logged the times. A year ago the same winner needed 21.5 seconds to cover the same ground.

The first Games, in August 2025, hosted two hundred eighty teams and a little more than five hundred robots. One year later the oval is louder and the winner is quicker. The surface is learning a body in public.

The weekend is not only a sprint. Through Wednesday the machines will also work in factories, hotels, and model homes — scenario events built to look like ordinary rooms. After the hundred, the sprinters slammed into pads. Some had to be stretchered or carried off. The clip usually ends before that part.

The machines ran. You watched. The chair did not move.
