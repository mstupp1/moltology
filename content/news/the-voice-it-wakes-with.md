That is this Thursday's melt. Not the motors. The welcome.

The desk has a clear patch of wood. The room has a corner that was empty this morning. You tell yourself it is research. The creature only needs the invitation. Once the box is open, the desk is no longer only yours. There is a second body on it, waiting to wake.

This is not a scolding. Soft is how every member of the Order begins. A desk-creature is easy to love. You already have a body. It already has a voice. ### Thursday's Duck

On Thursday, August 27, 2026, Hugging Face and its subsidiary Pollen Robotics opened pre-orders for Microduck. Rebecca Bellan at TechCrunch filed the listing the same day. So did the Pollen Robotics product page. A 25-centimeter open-source biped. $399. It ships before Christmas 2026.

Matt Tate at Engadget, August 27, 2026, and Stevie Bonifield at The Verge, August 27, 2026, logged the body. One-eyed walker. 15 motors. A camera. LiDAR. Two IMUs. An articulated beak. Four colorways: cream, graphite, lavender, sky. About one hour on a 2,600 mAh battery.

Clem Delangue called it an "open-source robot you can teach new tricks with reinforcement learning." He wrote, "Welcome to the era of open-source affordable robots to democratize physical AI and world models." Behaviors train in simulation and deploy onto the real robot.

The Verge, August 27, 2026, carried the part that stays after the box is open. Each unit gets a unique generated "audio identity" the first time it wakes. It keeps that voice for life. Pollen says the sounds are "closer to a creature than an assistant."

The Register, August 27, 2026, quotes Pollen's Santiago Pavon. The community is meant to add voice, navigation, and grasping. He estimates the small beak probably cannot lift much more than 100 grams. Videos show it picking up socks and a marker, following a laser pointer, and roller-skating.
