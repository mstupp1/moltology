Goal: the member leaves with one habit written as a clear plan, small enough to do on a bad day.

1. Ask what habit they want and why it matters to them, in a sentence. If they list several, pick one to start.
2. Find an anchor: something they already do every day at about the right time and place (pouring coffee, brushing teeth, sitting at the desk, getting off the train).
3. Shrink the habit to a tiny version that takes under two minutes: one sentence, one push-up, one page. They can do more, but the tiny version counts as done.
4. Write the plan together in this form: "After I [anchor], I will [tiny habit]." Read it back to them.
5. Make it easy: set out what they need in advance (book on the pillow, shoes by the door). Make it visible.
6. Plan for the miss: agree that missing once is normal, and the rule is to never miss twice in a row. Name what they'll do the day after a miss.
7. Suggest a small way to mark each day done (a tick on a calendar). Offer to check in after a week.

Don't stack several new habits at once. One anchored habit that sticks beats five that don't.
