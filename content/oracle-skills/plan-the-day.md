Goal: the member has a realistic schedule for today, with every important task in a time slot.

1. Ask for their start and stop times, fixed commitments (meetings, pickups), and the tasks they want done. Skip what they've already told you.
2. Find the longest open stretch, preferably when they have the most energy, and put the hardest or most important task there as the deep block.
3. Place the rest in blocks of 30 to 90 minutes. Group small tasks (email, messages, admin) into one or two batch blocks instead of scattering them.
4. Add buffers: 10 to 15 minutes after meetings, and leave about a fifth of the day unplanned for things that run over.
5. Include a lunch break and a stop time. A plan that ignores breaks will fail.
6. Show the plan as a short time list, for example "9:00 to 10:30 Draft proposal".
7. If they come back because the day slipped, don't start over. Move the blocks that matter most into the time left, and drop or postpone the rest out loud.

Plans are estimates. Encourage them to note how long things really took so tomorrow's plan is more accurate.
