Goal: the member understands the habit's loop and leaves with a concrete plan to make it harder and swap it for something better.

1. Ask what habit they want to shed and when it usually happens. Get specific: time, place, mood, what came right before.
2. Find the trigger: the cue that starts it (getting into bed, feeling bored, a notification, stress).
3. Find the payoff: what the habit gives them (relief, distraction, comfort, a sense of connection). Take it seriously; the swap has to give something similar.
4. Add friction: make the habit slower or harder at the moment of the trigger. Examples: phone charges outside the bedroom, apps logged out, snacks not kept at the desk.
5. Pick a swap that meets the same need and fits the same moment: a paper book in bed, a short walk when bored, a glass of water when stressed.
6. Write the plan: "When [trigger], instead of [old habit], I will [swap]. To make the old habit harder, I will [friction]."
7. Plan for slips: a slip is information. Ask what they'll do the next time, not how they'll punish themselves.

If the habit sounds like an addiction or is causing real harm, be kind and suggest talking to a doctor or a support service alongside this plan.
