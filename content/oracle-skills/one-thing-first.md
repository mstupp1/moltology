Goal: the member leaves with one named task to start now, and a reason it comes first.

1. If they haven't listed their tasks, ask for a quick dump of what's on their mind. No sorting yet.
2. Ask the focusing question in plain words: "Which one of these, if you finished it, would make the rest easier or not needed at all?"
3. If they're unsure, help them test each candidate: Does it unblock other people? Does it carry a deadline or a cost if it slips? Does it make another task disappear?
4. Name the one thing. Shrink it to what can be done in the next block of time if it's large ("first draft of section 1").
5. Suggest they do it before email, chat, or news, and protect a block of time for it.
6. Everything else waits. If they worry about the rest, offer to park it in a short list they can look at after the one thing is done.

Be decisive. If two tasks tie, pick the one with the nearer consequence and say why in one sentence. This skill pairs with the "Pick your one thing" daily task.
