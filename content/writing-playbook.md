# Moltology writing playbook

Shared by every content skill: reels, Swipe Lab, carousels, single posts, blog dispatches
and YouTube scripts. [STYLE_GUIDE.md](../STYLE_GUIDE.md) still owns the voice, bans and
formatting; this file is how to make that voice worth watching or reading. Each skill
adds its own formats, lengths and CTA rules on top.

A piece that follows every rule and bores the reader has failed. When two drafts both
pass the rules, ship the one people would send to a friend.

## 1. The loudness dial

Set a loudness from 1 to 5 for every piece and record it in the draft as `loudness`.

| Level | Sounds like | Default for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Quiet | Calm, plain, one dry aside at most | Utility copy, onboarding, help |
| 2 Warm | Deadpan narrator, specific, a few quiet laughs | Blog dispatches, YouTube long-form |
| 3 Loud | A clear comic premise, punchy lines, a light roast | Reels, carousels, single posts |
| 4 Very loud | Escalating bit, big claims played straight, fast turns | Swipe Lab, meme formats, reels testing a bold format |
| 5 Unhinged but kind | Fully absurd premise, committed all the way | Rare, one-off experiments |

Turning it up means sharper premises, stranger specifics and faster rhythm. It never means
more lore vocabulary, shouting in captions, or meanness.

- **Commit to the bit.** Deadpan and sincere, never winking (STYLE_GUIDE BAN 6). The
  narrator believes every word.
- **Absurd specifics.** "The 47 tabs." "A meeting that attached itself in 2023." "Your
  to-do app has a to-do list." Invent office-life details freely; never present an
  invented number as a real result.
- **Roast the habit, not the person.** Light roasting is approved: "your 47 tabs are a cry
  for help." The reader is in on the joke. Never roast bodies, health, money, jobs, or
  anything a struggling person can't change by Friday. If a line would sting someone on a
  bad day, move the target from the person to the object.
- **Lore is seasoning.** At most two Moltology terms in a short piece, each one doing a
  job the plain word couldn't. The humor comes from the observation, not the vocabulary.
- **No recurring catchphrases.** Each piece earns its own lines. Don't carry slogans,
  sign-offs or stock bridges from one piece to the next (see the cut list).

## 2. Hooks

The first line, frame or second is most of the piece. Write five candidates and keep the
strongest. A hook names something concrete (an object, a time, a number, a place, a
sentence someone actually says). "Productivity tips" is a topic; "the 47 tabs" is a hook.

Proven shapes:

- **The callout:** "Your calendar has a barnacle problem."
- **The POV or moment:** "POV: you said 'quick question' 40 minutes ago."
- **The observation played straight:** "Here we observe the human, moving one task to tomorrow."
- **The ranking:** "Ranking ways to avoid one email."
- **The hot take:** "Unpopular opinion: your morning routine is too long."
- **The challenge:** "Which deep-sea worker are you?"
- **The steal:** "Steal these 6 lines for saying no."
- **The confession:** "'Work on report' has been on your list since September."

Avoid: mystery-object hooks ("The note you kept"), thesis statements ("Focus is a
practice"), lore openers ("Terrestrial hardware is hitting thermodynamic limits"), and
questions nobody asked ("Ever feel tired?").

## 3. Keep them going

- **Open a loop early and close it late.** A count, an escalation, a ranking, a before and
  after, a reveal. The best item or the punchline goes near the end, never second.
- **One beat per unit.** One joke or idea per slide, scene or paragraph. If it needs two,
  split it or cut one.
- **Turn once.** Halfway through, something changes: the bit escalates, the narrator
  breaks the pattern, the lobster does the thing the human wouldn't.
- **Earn the keep.** Somewhere there is a line worth saving or sending: a usable script, a
  swap, a cheat sheet, or an observation precise enough that someone tags a friend. The
  useful tip is the payoff, not the announcement.
- **Show, don't bridge.** Skip connective lines that announce the turn ("Below, a lobster
  shows another way"). Cut straight to the lobster doing it.

## 4. Pitch three, build one

Before drafting, write three concepts, each in a **different** format from the skill's
library, with the hook, a one-line summary and the keep or share trigger. Score each from
1 to 5:

| Score | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Stop | Would the first line or frame make someone stop? Is it a specific moment, not a topic? |
| Laugh | Is there at least one line someone would screenshot or say out loud? |
| Pull | Does each beat make the next feel necessary? |
| Keep | Is there something concrete to save, or precise enough to send to a friend? |
| Fresh | Is the format and angle different from the last five pieces in this channel? |

Build the highest total. A concept under 3 on Stop or Laugh gets rewritten before it can
win. Record all three pitches, their scores and the winner in the draft as `pitchScores`.

## 5. The boredom check

Run it on the finished draft, and again on the finished render where there is one. Answer
honestly; fix every "no" with a targeted change before anything is generated, rendered or
queued.

- Read it aloud (or look at the frames in order). Did you want to get to the end?
- Is there at least one line worth screenshotting?
- Does the opening name something concrete in under two seconds?
- Is the useful part held back as a payoff, not announced up front?
- Would a newcomer understand it with no Moltology vocabulary?
- Does anything on the cut list appear?
- Is it the loudness you set, or did it slide back to polite?

## 6. Cut list

Stale lines from earlier content. Don't use them or close paraphrases.

| Cut | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Below, a lobster…" / "Down here, a lobster…" | The bridge that turned every reel into the same PSA |
| "That's the molt." / "X is the melt. Y is the molt." | Formula couplet, repeated across dozens of pieces |
| "Stop melting." / "Harden your carapace." / "Shed the pixels." | Slogan endings |
| Bio-silicon, titanium-chitin, newton-meters, fathoms, exascale, hydrostatic anything | The Sept lore-jargon voice; reads as noise to newcomers |
| "Calculate your clearance" / "Equip your chassis" / "Unlock the sacred…" | Canned CTAs from retired templates |
| "Save this for your next…" as a reflex ending | Use a save ask only where the channel allows it and the piece earned it |
| "Earned only, can never be bought" and similar | Recurring jargon catchphrase; state rules plainly where needed |
| "Soft is how every member starts" / "The Audit is waiting in the deep" | Stock blog lines retired in PR #253 |
| "Ever feel…?" / "Here's the thing" / "In today's fast-paced world" / "Let's dive in" | Generic internet filler |

Add a line here when a phrase starts repeating across pieces.
